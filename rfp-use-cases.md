# RFP Agent — Use Cases

## 1. Bid / No-Bid Decision

The first thing that happens when an RFP arrives. Should we respond at all?

### Strategic Alignment
- Does this opportunity align with our strategic goals and focus areas?
- Does it fit our target market, industry, or geography?
- Would winning this strengthen or dilute our positioning?

### Capability & Skill Fit
- Do we have the technical capabilities and domain expertise required?
- Are there requirements we can't meet or would need to subcontract?
- Gap analysis: what's missing vs. what's table stakes vs. nice-to-have?

### Resource Availability
- Do we have the people to both write the response and execute the work?
- What's the opportunity cost — what else would we not be doing?
- Timeline feasibility: can we realistically meet the submission deadline?

### Win Probability
- Who are the likely competitors and how do we stack up?
- Do we have an existing relationship with this customer?
- Is the RFP wired for an incumbent or is it genuinely open?
- Are evaluation criteria transparent and favorable to our strengths?

### Past Performance
- Have we won similar RFPs before? What was our track record?
- Do we have relevant case studies, references, and past deliverables?
- What can we learn from previous losses on similar opportunities?

### Profitability & ROI
- Rough estimate of deal value vs. cost to pursue and deliver
- Pricing pressure: is this a race to the bottom or value-based?
- Long-term revenue potential (renewals, follow-on work, expansion)

### Output
Structured go/no-go recommendation with scoring across dimensions, key risks, and conditions (e.g., "go if we can partner with X for capability Y").

## 2. Response Strategy

Once we decide to respond, define the overall approach before writing begins.

### Win Themes
- What are our 3-5 key differentiators for this specific opportunity?
- What pain points does the customer have that we uniquely solve?
- What story are we telling — why us, why now?

### Customer Insights
- What do we know about this customer (industry, challenges, priorities)?
- Any intel from past interactions, news, earnings calls, strategic plans?
- How do we personalize the response to resonate with their context?

### Team Assembly
- **Response team**: who writes which sections? Who reviews?
- **Proposed execution team**: key personnel, resumes, org chart
- **Subject matter experts**: who do we pull in for specific questions?

### Competitive Positioning
- Anticipated competitors and their likely angles
- How do we differentiate without being negative?
- Pricing strategy relative to expected competition

## 3. Requirements Extraction & Outline

Parse the RFP and build the skeleton of the response.

### Requirements Extraction
- Pull out every requirement, question, and evaluation criterion
- Classify: mandatory vs. preferred vs. informational
- Flag ambiguities or contradictions to clarify with the issuer
- Map requirements to compliance matrix (comply / partial / exception)

### Response Outline
- Build section-by-section structure mirroring the RFP's requested format
- Assign owners and deadlines per section
- Identify which sections need original writing vs. reuse from knowledge base
- Note page limits, formatting requirements, and submission instructions

---

## 4. First Draft Generation

Produce an initial draft by pulling from knowledge base and generating new content.

### Knowledge Base Search
- For each requirement/question, search past proposals, boilerplate, case studies
- Retrieve relevant technical documentation, product specs, process descriptions
- Pull in approved company language (about us, capabilities, certifications)

### Draft Assembly
- Generate section drafts combining retrieved content with new writing
- Tailor boilerplate to this specific customer and opportunity
- Fill in specifics: names, dates, project references, metrics
- Draft compliance matrix responses

---

## 5. Iterative Refinement (Human-in-the-Loop)

Collaborative editing cycle between AI and human reviewers.

### Section-Level Review
- SMEs review and correct technical content
- Capture reviewers' edits and preferences to improve future drafts
- Track what's approved vs. still in progress

### Cross-Section Consistency
- Ensure terminology, acronyms, and messaging are consistent throughout
- Reconcile conflicting statements across sections from different authors
- Verify all cross-references are accurate

### Attachment & Collateral Generation
- Resumes / key personnel bios
- Org charts and staffing plans
- Project schedules and Gantt charts
- Architecture diagrams and technical illustrations
- Pricing tables and cost breakdowns
- Letters of commitment, teaming agreements

---

## 6. Quality Control Gates

Systematic checks before submission.

### Compliance Check
- Every mandatory requirement addressed? Compliance matrix complete?
- All submission instructions followed (format, page count, sections, copies)?
- All requested attachments and forms included?

### Cohesiveness & Tone
- Does it read as one unified document or a patchwork?
- Is the tone appropriate (professional, confident, not arrogant)?
- Do win themes come through consistently across sections?

### Security & Compliance
- No disclosure of proprietary information we shouldn't share
- Pricing doesn't expose internal cost structures inappropriately
- Complies with any regulatory requirements (ITAR, HIPAA, etc.)
- No PII or sensitive data in the wrong places

### Branding & Formatting
- Consistent with brand guidelines (fonts, colors, logo usage)
- Professional formatting, clean layout, no orphaned headers
- Table of contents, page numbers, headers/footers correct

### Executive Review
- Final sign-off from leadership on strategy, pricing, and commitments
- Legal review of terms, exceptions, and representations

---

## 7. Analysis & Insight

Cross-cutting capabilities that support multiple stages above.

### Risk & Gap Analysis
- Technical risks: what could go wrong in delivery?
- Compliance gaps: where do we fall short and what's our mitigation?
- Dependencies: subcontractors, technology, timeline assumptions

### ROI & Pricing Analysis
- Cost modeling: labor, materials, travel, overhead, margin
- Sensitivity analysis: what if scope grows, timeline slips, rates change?
- Comparison to similar past projects (actuals vs. estimates)

### Executive Summary Generation
- Synthesize the entire response into a compelling 1-2 page overview
- Lead with customer problem, our solution, why we're the best fit
- Include key metrics, differentiators, and a clear call to confidence

### Customer Intelligence
- Aggregate everything known about the customer into a briefing
- Recent news, organizational changes, stated priorities
- History with us: past projects, satisfaction, relationship health
- Use insights to personalize language, examples, and emphasis throughout
